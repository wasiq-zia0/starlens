Protecting Canada

Guarding the roof
of the world.

Forty per cent of Canada lies in the Arctic, behind three‑quarters of its coastline. As the ice opens, the North fills with ships, sensors and satellites — and a nation that cannot move its own data across its own Arctic does not fully command it.

Sovereign by design

Canadian ground, Canadian hands.

Sensitive data comes home through Canadian soil — no foreign relay, no borrowed backbone. The station deploys, operates and is maintained by Canadians, wherever Canada needs to listen.

Physics as armour

A needle of light.

A radio field can be found and flooded. A laser link is a beam metres wide after a four‑hundred‑kilometre journey, aimed at one point on Earth — there is no field to jam and almost nothing to detect.

Presence is protection

Sovereignty you can carry.

Every deployment extends the country's reach: over sea ice in Nunavut this month, a gravel strip on Ellesmere the next. Where the station stands, Canada is connected — and seen to be.

And when Canada's quantum satellites fly, Arctic‑4 will be waiting under the aurora — ready to catch keys no adversary can copy.
